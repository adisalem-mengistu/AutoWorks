STARK Hackathon – Team Progress & Project Direction
1. Team Formation
Our team, sudoSmashIt, started discussing ideas for the STARK Hackathon on September 13, 2026.
The initial discussion focused on understanding the hackathon requirements and deciding what type of real-world problem we should solve.
The team agreed that we should avoid choosing an idea randomly and instead identify a meaningful problem that could have a practical impact in Ethiopia.
2. Initial Discussion: Choosing a Domain
At the beginning, the team discussed whether we should first choose a specific domain or directly start generating project ideas.
Possible domains mentioned included:
E-commerce
Medicine / Pharmacy
Local services
Other real-world community problems
The team agreed that choosing a domain first could help us narrow the problem space and develop a more focused solution.
We also discussed important qualities for the project, including:
Real-life usefulness
Accessibility
Security
Support for local users
Amharic and English language preferences
A clear problem that people actually experience
3. Understanding the STARK Hackathon
On September 14, the team shared and reviewed the official STARK Hackathon website and requirements.
The team began checking:
Hackathon requirements
Build/start dates
How projects should be submitted
The role of GitHub
The STARK platform and ecosystem
What could be built using STARK
The team recognized that the project should not only be technically interesting but should also fit the hackathon requirements and demonstrate a useful real-world application.
4. Idea Generation
After discussing the direction, the team created a poll on September 15 to choose a project idea and start development immediately.
The proposed ideas were:
E-commerce for local businesses

Digetegna Waga Chimary tracking
Job market for technicians
The team was encouraged to select one idea and begin implementation rather than spending too much time discussing possibilities.
5. Current Idea Exploration: Technician Job Market
One of the ideas receiving attention is a job market / service marketplace for technicians.
The general concept is to create a platform where people who need technical or household services can find suitable technicians more easily.
Possible services could include:
Plumbing
Electrical work
Carpentry
Mechanics
Appliance repair
Satellite installation
Other local technical services
The problem being explored is that many people still depend heavily on word-of-mouth recommendations when looking for trustworthy technicians.
A digital platform could make it easier to:
Find nearby service providers
View technician profiles
Compare available providers
Understand services offered
Contact technicians
Build trust through verification and reviews
6. Existing Solutions Research
Before committing to the technician marketplace idea, the team started researching existing platforms to determine whether the idea already exists and how our solution could be different.
Examples discussed include:
Balemuya Home & Handyman Services Ethiopia
A local service marketplace connecting customers with technicians such as plumbers, electricians, mechanics, satellite installers, and carpenters.
The platform focuses on technician profiles, identity verification, credential checking, and secure payments.
TaskMoby
An on-demand marketplace connecting households with professional maintenance workers.
Services include plumbing, electrical repairs, cleaning, and other home maintenance tasks.
Jiji Ethiopia
A classifieds platform where independent service providers can advertise their services and customers can contact them directly.
Serrale
A local service-provider directory connecting residents with professionals such as plumbers, electricians, carpenters, and appliance repair workers.
Manofix Addis Ababa
A platform for finding people who provide handyman and repair services in Addis Ababa.
7. Important Observation
The team identified that the technician/service marketplace concept is not completely new.
[9/15/2026 8:48 PM] Adiss M: Existing platforms already provide similar services.
Therefore, simply creating another platform for finding plumbers or electricians would not be enough.
The team needs to identify a specific gap or problem that existing platforms do not solve well.
This is now an important part of our idea validation process.
Possible areas for differentiation include:
Better accessibility for local users
Amharic/English support
Improved trust and verification
Better technician matching
Transparent pricing
Location-based matching
Service quality tracking
Reputation/history of technicians
Better protection for both customers and technicians
A feature specifically designed around an Ethiopian/local problem

9. Our Current Direction
At this stage, the team is moving from general brainstorming → problem validation → solution selection.
The current strongest direction being explored is a platform related to local technicians and service providers, but the team has not yet finalized it.
The next step is not simply to copy an existing service marketplace. Instead, we need to answer:
What specific problem do Ethiopian customers and technicians face that existing platforms do not solve effectively?
Once this is identified, we can define our unique solution and determine how STARK can be meaningfully integrated.
10. Next Steps
